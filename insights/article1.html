Colocation Pricing 2026: What a Rack Really Costs by Market

What does a rack cost in 2026? Colocation pricing per kW and per rack across Ashburn, Dallas, Chicago, and London — plus the hidden fees inflating quotes.

PRIME_DOMAIN_CAPITAL_INSIGHTS · 2026-10-17 · colocation pricing 2026

A rack is a rack is a rack — until you price one in Ashburn versus Dallas and find a spread wide enough to fund a headcount. In 2026, colocation pricing has become a study in contradictions: record construction, record demand, and prices rising in nearly every major market. Understanding what a rack actually costs now requires reading past the headline rate, because the headline rate is the least honest number on the quote.

Start with the macro picture from CBRE's North American Data Center Trends report, published August 2026. New construction rose almost 25% in the first half of 2026 across the major markets. Total power capacity grew by a third year over year. And vacancy — the share of power capacity available for lease — fell to a record low of 1.4%. As CBRE's Pat Lynch put it: it is unlike any other industry in his lifetime, where you can triple supply and still watch vacancy rates go down. When there is nothing to lease, what exists gets expensive.

The numbers, market by market

For standard retail colocation, published list prices still anchor the low end of the conversation. Providers such as Psychz Networks and ServerMania listed a full 42U cabinet from roughly $599 to $1,500 per month in early 2026, depending on power allocation — a 1.92 kW cabinet around $608/month, a 5 kW cabinet around $1,058/month, each plus a $500 setup fee on a one-year term. These are single-provider list prices, not market averages, and they describe low-density, commodity space. The moment you need real power density in a real metro, the numbers move.

Broker-quoted 2026 market ranges for full cabinets in major metros run $1,500 to $2,325 per month — Buffalo at the low end, New York at the high end. In London, full-cabinet rates run roughly £1,800 to £3,800 per month in the Slough financial corridor, £1,600 to £3,400 in Docklands, and £1,100 to £2,200 in Royal Docks and outer London, all excluding VAT. None of the major London operators publish fixed prices; every quote is negotiated on rack count, density, connectivity, and term.

High-density and AI-ready space is priced per kilowatt-month, and this is where the market map really matters. Per 2026 broker reporting: Northern Virginia (Ashburn) $158–$203/kW-month for a 10 kW rack, with GPU-specific colocation quoted at $180–$300/kW-month and a market vacancy of roughly 0.3% in Q1 2026 — effectively a closed market for large deployments, with 6-to-12-month queues. Dallas runs $158–$203 at 10 kW and $135–$179 at 50 kW, widely considered the best cost-to-availability balance in the US right now. Silicon Valley and Los Angeles sit at $179–$225. Chicago runs $179–$230 at 10 kW with vacancy near 2.2%. Atlanta $158–$203 with vacancy around 1.0%. Phoenix $158–$203, increasingly positioned as California's plan B. Seattle and the Pacific Northwest $158–$203, with Washington State at the lower end on cheap hydro power. The Dakotas $100–$170 — the lowest power cost in the country, with limited carrier depth. Secondary markets like Colorado have seen quotes as low as $78/kW base for multi-hundred-kW commitments.

At the top of the density curve, AI-ready colocation in Ashburn is market-reported at roughly $180/kW-month for air-cooled and $225/kW-month for liquid-cooled at 100 kW-plus per rack. CBRE's rental-rate data tells the same story in operator terms: Northern Virginia $190–$235/kW-month, Chicago $200–$230 (up 14.7% year over year, the steepest increase in North America), Atlanta up 2%, Dallas–Fort Worth unchanged.

What the headline rate doesn't include

The per-kW quote is the beginning of the invoice, not the end. Broker analysis consistently finds that power billing model, cross-connects, and remote hands add 25–30% to the real monthly cost — the full range runs 20–60% depending on connectivity requirements. A liquid-cooling premium of $1,000–$2,000 per rack-month sits on top of the power rate. Space and standard cross-connects are frequently billed separately from power: $1,200–$3,500 per rack-month for space alone in retail structures.

Do the arithmetic on a real rack. A 135 kW rack at $200 per kW-month is $27,000 a month in power alone — before space, before the liquid premium, before a single cross-connect. At that scale, a 10% difference in the power rate is worth more than most one-time concessions, and the liquid-cooling premium becomes a rounding error. Model the whole stack per rack per month, then compare. Anyone comparing single headline numbers across providers with different bundling is comparing nothing.

Two structural facts shape every negotiation in 2026. First, power billing model matters enormously: flat-rate plans offer predictability; metered plans charge actual draw. With AI workloads, the difference between committed and consumed power is real money, and the contract's treatment of burst, overage, and power factor will move the effective rate more than the headline. Second, term and timing are leverage. Operators with near-zero vacancy concede little; brokers with 25-year operator relationships report negotiating two to three free months at term start — but only where capacity exists to negotiate over. In Ashburn at 0.3% vacancy, there is no negotiation. There is a queue.

Why prices rise when supply is surging

The paradox resolves when you look at what is being built. The construction boom is dominated by hyperscaler self-build and pre-leased wholesale capacity — space that never reaches the retail market. What reaches the market faces grid-connection timelines of 24 to 48 months in many metros, equipment lead times, and a power constraint that CBRE has named the industry's top challenge for three consecutive years. Supply is growing; leasable supply is not.

For buyers, the implication is practical. Price the power, not the rack. Get three quotes on an identical per-kW-month basis with identical assumptions about billing model, cross-connects, and cooling. And treat market selection as the first and largest pricing decision: the gap between the Bay Area and the Dakotas at 50 kW is roughly $100 per kW-month — $60,000 a month on a 600 kW deployment. In 2026, nobody buys racks. They buy megawatts with a lease attached.
