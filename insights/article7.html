Data Center Relocation Cost: What Moving a Data Center Really Costs

Data center relocation cost in 2026: about $10,000 per rack on average, $50,000–$150,000 for a server room under 10 racks, $200,000–$750,000 for 10–50 racks — the full breakdown, plus the downtime that dwarfs the moving bill.

PRIME_DOMAIN_CAPITAL_INSIGHTS · 2026-11-14 · data center relocation cost

The trucks were the cheapest line on the invoice. That is the first thing a CIO learns from moving a data center, and the second is that the quote for the move was never the budget for the move. A relocation is priced in racks and paid for in downtime, overlap rent, and a new colocation contract that runs three to five years. The physical move is the visible event. Discovery, overlap, validation, and transition risk are much of the economic project — and the figures below separate the moving bill from the bill that follows it.

What a move costs, by size

The most cited benchmark in the industry comes from Info-Tech Research Group: an average relocation of about $120,000, or about $10,000 per rack, based on its survey of relocation budgets. 2026 estimates from relocation specialists bracket that figure rather than confirming it. Relocation Strategies puts a server room under 10 racks at $50,000–$150,000, a 10–50 rack move at $200,000–$750,000, and a large consolidation at $1 million and up. Specialty Transport Solutions, a carrier that has run more than 500 data center relocations, ranges small moves of 5–20 racks at $15,000–$75,000, mid-size moves of 20–100 racks at $75,000–$400,000, and large enterprise moves of 100-plus racks at $400,000 into the millions. A May 2026 specialist guide gives a physical-relocation range of $5,000–$25,000 per rack. Those numbers do not average into a single industry figure — the dates, samples, and cost boundaries are not aligned — and any article that blends them into one average is inventing precision. Use them as a range, and budget to the top of it.

Timeline runs with size. A small server room of 1–5 racks takes 6–8 weeks end to end; a mid-market move runs 8–16 weeks from requirements to stable cutover, longer with a cloud migration or hardware refresh attached. A Hong Kong benchmark from Brocent, a relocation firm, puts a 1–5 rack move at HK$150,000–$300,000 and 10–30 racks at HK$300,000–$800,000 — the same shape as the US ranges, in a market with some of the highest power costs in Asia-Pacific.

The lines the quote leaves out

Overlap is the first. Paying for both sites runs 30–90 days on a typical move, and it is one of the most negotiable costs in the new contract — negotiate it before signing, not after. The new contract is the second, and the largest: the three-to-five-year colocation agreement at the destination is set by the market you choose and the terms you negotiate, which is why a migration is decided at the contract, not on moving day. Destination rents in primary North American markets average about $194.95 per kW per month — the full picture is in our colocation pricing analysis.

Then the small lines that add up. Network cabling at the new site runs $125–$300 per installed data drop. IT labor for a small migration runs $5,000–$10,000 before cabling. Cross-connect fees, new circuit installs, and re-certification follow. And the line that dwarfs them all: downtime. Specialty Transport Solutions puts the average cost of unplanned downtime at about $9,000 per minute — a two-hour outage caused by a migration error costs $1,080,000. That is why live or phased migrations cost more than a single cold move and are still the right choice for anything that cannot stop.

Relocate or modernize?

Every relocation quote should be read against a modernization quote, because the two solve the same problem — an estate that no longer fits — and modernization often costs less. Modernizing in place means refreshing cooling and power distribution, replacing end-of-life hardware, consolidating racks, and renegotiating the existing contract, without moving a single server. It wins when the lease has term left, the building can take more power, and the constraint is efficiency rather than capacity. Relocation wins when the lease is expiring, the site cannot get more power, the market at the destination is cheaper, or the business needs a different geography — national vacancy at 1.4%, a historic low, has pushed more than one tenant to a power-rich secondary market for exactly that reason. The honest comparison prices both options over the same three-to-five-year window, including the new contract on one side and the refresh cycle on the other. A move that looks expensive against a modernization quote can look cheap against five years of rent in the wrong market — and the reverse is just as common.

The estimate that holds

A defensible relocation estimate starts with a site survey and a rack-by-rack migration runbook, not a per-rack formula. The runbook — discovery, risk mapping, licensed transport, validated cutover — is what turns a $10,000-per-rack average into a number a CFO can sign. Get three quotes against the same scope, require the overlap terms in writing, and price the phased option alongside the cold move. The cheapest quote is usually the one that left out the lines above.

Moving a data center costs $10,000 a rack on average and anywhere from $5,000 to $25,000 a rack in practice, $50,000 to $750,000 for most mid-size moves, and $1 million-plus for a consolidation — before the new rent, before the overlap, and before the downtime that a bad cutover can add in a single afternoon. Budget the project, the contract, and the risk together, or the trucks will be the only line that came in on budget.
